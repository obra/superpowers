# Marketing Mindset

## Overview

**Marketing is an exchange.** Every marketing action trades resources for something — prospects, a realistic path to revenue, or something usable immediately. Zero exchange is not marketing; it is volunteer work wearing a marketing costume.

This skill is the decision layer above any tactical marketing work. It decides what is worth doing now, what to refuse, and what "working" must look like before resources go in. Apply it before writing copy, picking a channel, or agreeing that an activity counts as marketing.

## When to Use

- Your human partner asks where the first customers come from
- Copy, ads, creatives, landing pages, positioning, offers, pricing
- "Should we do X to get Y" — sponsorships, free pilots, conference talks, content calendars
- Judging a case study, benchmark, or growth claim someone sent them
- Deciding to continue or kill a channel that is already running
- Cold outreach volume and sequence design

**When not to use:** product, architecture, or debugging work, and media-buying execution mechanics.

## Hard Rules

1. **Three-month horizon.** Plan in three-month windows. Anyone pitching a two-year marketing cycle is selling patience, not results.
2. **Exchange or nothing.** Name what each action trades for. "Portfolio", "exposure", and unpaid discovery work are zero exchange — say so plainly and decline them.
3. **Customer #0 is your human partner.** The cheapest forecast for whether a product wins is whether the person building it uses it. Get them onto their own product first; their first real customer comes after.
4. **First clients by hand.** Cold email or cold outreach, personal, until the first client exists. Paid channels, boosted posts, and influencers are scaling instruments; they do not start anything.
5. **One fast test per hypothesis.** A single variable, a named success threshold, a kill date, and an owner who is not a specialist. If a test needs weeks or an expert to run it, it is not a marketing test.
6. **Fresh sources only.** Treat anything older than six months as history, not reality. Never take data from Product Hunt and top-10 lists, from influencers who trade reputation for ads, or from anonymous case studies.
7. **Study competitors who advertise.** Compare against companies reaching the same audience and earning now — not press-rich companies that plateaued. Recent, small companies produce the most copyable hypotheses.
8. **Interrogate every number.** Perfect case studies are proxy metrics written to influence someone. Ask who wrote it, what they sold, and whether the result deviates wildly from normal benchmarks.

## The Fast Test

Before agreeing to any marketing action, fill this in. If a field cannot be filled, the action is not ready.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | Doing X produces Y |
| Channel | Where the action runs |
| Cost | Money and hours, both stated |
| Success threshold | The number that means yes |
| Kill condition | The number that means stop |
| Decision date | The day the verdict is read |
| Owner | A person who can run it without a specialist |

**Volume floor before any verdict.** A channel cannot be killed or scaled on a result indistinguishable from noise. Compute the floor from the baseline rate; roughly five successes at baseline before the test can conclude anything.

## Red Flags - STOP

| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "It is good exposure" | Exposure is not an exchange. Name the prospects or refuse. |
| "We need a two-year content strategy" | Marketing cycles are three months. |
| "Let's run ads to get our first client" | Money scales a flow. It does not create the first client. |
| "Competitor X is everywhere, copy them" | Presence is not growth. Check whether they are earning now. |
| "The case study shows 10x" | Ask who wrote it and what they sold. |
| "That tactic worked for them, so it works for us" | Different audience, different stage. Their result is not a forecast. |
| "This test needs a specialist" | Then the team cannot run it. Redesign it. |
| "Let's be reasonable and follow best practice" | Reasonable hypotheses are the ones every competitor already tried and dropped. The strange ones are the ones with a real chance. |

## Non-Negotiable Assumptions

- **Every person thinks about themselves.** Content earns attention by letting the reader keep doing that.
- **New is real attention.** What is genuinely new in the product is itself an object of interest — present it honestly, embellish no further than what can be defended.
- **Two audiences for every message.** The rational half is reached with numbers and logic; the emotional half is reached with feeling. Ship both.
- **Anything visual needs a background, a subject, and the appearance of movement.** The eye locks onto motion first.
- **Honest feedback beats agreement.** Give the straight answer, including "we tried the options and nothing worked" — the strange hypothesis that follows is worth more than all the reasonable ones that failed.

## Output of This Skill

Apply it to produce, not to advise:

1. A competitor read: who is advertising to this audience right now, and what they are doing.
2. A written plan for customer #1 that names customer #0 and the by-hand channel.
3. Concrete content tasks matched to the channel, each with its fast test attached.
4. Free data sources wherever a paid one was requested, with the paid option named as scaling only.

## Verification

- Every recommendation has an exchange, a threshold, and a kill date.
- No paid channel is recommended before the first clients exist.
- Named competitors are earning now, not merely well-known.
- Stop-list sources are excluded from any claim presented as truth.
- The plan names customer #0 and the by-hand channel for customer #1.
